Any medical sensor, ours or another maker's, connects through one API, and the platform compares each astronaut with themselves, on board and with no link to Earth. We built this project in Quemchi, in Chiloé. In Chile, a nursing technician runs the rural health post day to day and the doctor comes on rounds; to the islands of Chiloé, the Navy patrol ship Cirujano Videla brings them. On the way to Mars there is no interplanetary Cirujano Videla, and the main problem is not measuring: it is the astronaut keeping quiet. In the Institute of Medicine's words, "the fear of mission disqualification increases their reluctance to report", and NASA acknowledged in 2023 that real cases may be underestimated for that reason. More sensors sending data to Earth do not fix that. Fixed limits also warn late: the VO2max limit fires when the capacity is already lost. And nobody arrives in time: up to 22 minutes per message, and two weeks without a link every two years. The crew carries a medical officer and medical equipment, but the officer is not a specialist physician and only knows what crewmates report. PuduHealth supports both: the astronaut and the medical officer.

Por eso hicimos lo contrario de lo habitual: en vez de mandar los datos del astronauta a la Tierra, se quedan con el astronauta, privados y cifrados, para que se atreva a contar lo que siente. La app le dice qué hacer, con la norma de NASA que lo respalda, y cuándo avisar al oficial médico. PuduHealth es una plataforma, no un sensor. Cualquier fuente entrega sus mediciones por una API abierta con un solo formato: una banda o un reloj con Bluetooth estándar, un termómetro, el wearable que diseñamos o un equipo de la nave. Como no teníamos hardware, construimos un adaptador con la cámara del celular, pero es solo una fuente más. El motor compara a cada persona con su propia línea base y decide con reglas citadas. Con datos reales de Fitbit de 14 personas, marca un alza sostenida de +5 lpm en 3 días, con alertas en el 2,5 % de los días comunes. En la misión de ejemplo escribimos un deterioro (desde el día 135 la comandante duerme y entrena menos) y sus primeros avisos llegan 55 días antes del límite de VO2max: ilustra el mecanismo, no es una medición.

So we did the opposite of the usual: instead of sending the astronaut's data to Earth, we leave it with them, private and encrypted, so they dare to say how they feel. The app tells them what to do, with the NASA standard behind it, and when to call the medical officer. PuduHealth is a platform, not a sensor. Any source delivers its measurements through one open API with a single format: a chest strap or watch with standard Bluetooth, a thermometer, the wearable we designed, or onboard equipment. We had no hardware, so we built an adapter with the phone camera, but it is just one more source. The engine compares each person with their own baseline and decides with cited rules. On real Fitbit data from 14 people, it flags a sustained +5 bpm rise in 3 days, with alerts on 2.5 % of ordinary days. In the example mission we scripted a decline (from day 135 the commander sleeps and trains less) and her first warnings arrive 55 days before the VO2max limit: it illustrates the mechanism, it is not a measurement.

Entrenamos un modelo de estrés agudo con electrocardiogramas de 15 personas: AUC 0,93 dejando a cada persona fuera, y corre en el teléfono con la frecuencia cardíaca de cualquier sensor. Calibramos el detector con datos reales de 14 personas durante cinco meses: alerta en el 2,5 % de los días y detecta un alza sostenida de cinco latidos en tres días. En infecciones reales, la frecuencia cardíaca sube dos latidos: por eso una alerta de infección exige dos señales. This is not an idea in the air. We trained an acute-stress model on ECG from 15 people: AUC 0.93 leaving each person out, and it runs on the phone with heart rate from any sensor. We calibrated the detector on real data from 14 people over five months: it alerts on 2.5 % of days and catches a sustained rise of five beats in three days. In real infections heart rate rises by two beats: that is why an infection alert needs two signals. The rules come from NASA-STD-3001 and OCHMO briefs, and the mission's radiation is what Curiosity's RAD instrument measured.
